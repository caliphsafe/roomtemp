A gathering for people with taste, curiosity, and something to express. Global sounds. Short live performances. Outfits worth remembering. Energy you can feel from across the room—or through the screen.

01 / DJ position

No distant booth.

A compact, protected setup at the center of the action. The audience surrounds the music, with clear space for equipment, cables, and artist handoffs.

The right kind of room

A bar, restaurant, or lounge with character. Flexible furniture, a workable sound system, reliable upload connectivity, and room for guests to move comfortably.

Capacity is confirmed after the production layout—not before it.

01 / How the room earns its temperature +

The proposed Room Temp reading is an editorial assessment by the team, not an automated crowd measurement. Four dimensions contribute up to 25 points each.

Movement / 25

Participation across the room, not only the people nearest the DJ.

Music / 25

Musical connection, responsive selection, and strong artist moments.

Connection / 25

Shared reactions, inclusion, and people engaging with one another.

Continuity / 25

Energy sustained across transitions rather than one isolated reaction.

97–100: the rare top zone. Requires at least 24/25 in every dimension, agreement from all three founders, and a review of the night's documented moments. It is a rare retrospective distinction. A full room, high volume, spending, or a big online audience alone cannot earn it.

During a future event, any displayed reading should be labeled a producer assessment. Post-event results can become an edition's “temperature report.” The deck's interactive scale is a demonstration only.

03 / Ownership, responsibilities & decisions +

Agree the ownership structure, founder compensation, expense approval, profit distribution, account access, and ownership of the name, footage, and audience relationships before taking payments.

Caliph owns creative direction; Fresco owns musical programming; Boogz owns broadcast and creator strategy. The producer coordinates budgets and delivery, while the technical lead owns production readiness. Major commitments and the rare top-tier award require shared founder agreement.

04 / Production, people & broadcast readiness +
  • Confirm occupancy for the final layout, clear exits, accessible circulation, cable protection, and equipment boundaries with the venue.
  • Agree staffing, security, artist fees, sound levels, hospitality, guest conduct, filming notices, and consent for featured portraits.
  • Test a clean DJ feed, live vocals, ambient crowd microphones, camera coverage, upload connection, and a local recording backup.
  • Confirm music, backing-track, performance, recording, replay, and promotional permissions. Check platform-specific sponsorship conditions before selling broadcast placements.
  • Prepare a fallback if the stream drops: keep the room running and record locally where permitted. Publish only footage and music cleared for that use.

Planning references: YouTube live-stream copyright · Twitch DJ program · Meta music guidelines

05 / Content, measurement & the next edition +

Define partner deliverables before the event: formats, quantities, usage rights, approval windows, delivery dates, and reporting. Avoid promising reach before it exists.

Review paid attendance, ticket conversion, actual event margin, stream watch time and peak concurrent viewers, artist feedback, guest return intent, content performance, and partner renewal interest. Keep online viewing figures separate from in-room attendance.

Capture what worked in the music, floor layout, transitions, and energy assessment. Repeat locally until the experience and economics are reliable, then adapt the format with trusted partners in another city.
